1
Squat Jumpsor fast stand-ups
4 × 5rest 60–90sBodyweight
Power, done fresh: explode up, soft landing, full reset each rep.
Feet shoulder-width. Squat to parallel, then drive explosively upward. Land softly with knees slightly bent and fully reset before the next rep.
2
Bulgarian Split Squatsrear foot raised
3 × 8 /legrest 60s4–7kg
3-sec descent, shin vertical, weight in front heel. Lean your torso slightly forward and drive through the front heel to bias the glutes over the quads.
Rest your rear foot on the bench behind you, front foot about a stride ahead. Lower your back knee toward the floor, keeping your front shin vertical. Drive through your front heel to stand.
3
Romanian Deadliftsdumbbells · heaviest
3 × 10rest 60s7–10kg · heaviest
Flat back always — never round the spine. Push your backside back, hinge at the hips, soft knees. Go light while your back settles and master the hinge first.
Hold DBs in front of your thighs. Push your hips back and hinge forward with a flat back — stop when you feel a hamstring stretch. Drive hips forward to stand, squeeze glutes at the top. If the lower back feels it at all, go lighter.
4
Glute Bridge → Hip Thrustfeet flat · DB on hips
3 × 12rest 60sBW or 7–10kg on hips
Direct glute work — no balancing. Squeeze hard at the top, drive through your heels, and tuck the hips so the lower back doesn't arch.
Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat about hip-width with heels close to your backside. Rest a dumbbell across your hips if using weight. Push through your heels and lift your hips until your body forms a straight line from knees to shoulders, squeezing the glutes hard at the top — tuck the pelvis slightly so the lower back stays flat, never arched. Lower with control. To progress, rest your upper back on a sofa or bench (hip thrust) for a bigger range of motion.
5
Single-Leg Calf Raisesoff a step
2 × 15 /legrest 45sBW or hold 4–7kg
Full range, hold wall for balance.
Stand on one foot on the edge of a step, heel hanging off. Lower your heel below the step, then rise up onto your tiptoe as high as possible. Touch the wall lightly for balance only.
6
Lying Leg Raisesside-lying · outer hip
2 × 15 /siderest 45sBodyweight
Straight-leg hip abduction — the side glute (glute medius), which the routine doesn't otherwise train directly. Lead with the heel, keep hips stacked.
Lie on your side with hips and shoulders stacked, legs stacked and mostly straight. Raise the top leg upward, leading with the heel and keeping it in line with your body — don't let it drift forward. Lower with control, stopping just short of touching the bottom leg. Keep the hips stacked throughout; don't roll backward to gain range.
Core finisher · every session · Plank 2 × 30s · Dead bug 2 × 8/side. Brace as if bracing for a punch; don't let the lower back arch.
Chest · arms · back-safe · weight only goes down · ~25–30 min
Warm-up · arm circles · 15 band pull-aparts · 10 slow push-ups
1
Dynamic-Effort Bench Presslight load, moved fast
4 × 5rest 60–90s4kg · light
Power, done fresh: light load, explosive drive up, controlled lower. Same "fast under load" effect the squat jumps give the legs — far less wrist stress than explosive push-ups.
Lie on the bench, DBs at chest height with elbows around 45°. Use a light-to-moderate weight — roughly 50–60% of your normal working weight. Press up as explosively as possible while staying controlled, then lower back to the chest under control. Reset fully between reps.
2
Bench Pressmain lift · heaviest
3 × 10rest 60s7–10kg
Control to chest, drive up. The bench keeps the back supported and gives a bit more range than the floor.
Lie on the bench, DBs at chest height with elbows at roughly 45°. Press up until arms are extended, then lower slowly back until DBs touch your chest.
3
DB Seated Shoulder Pressback against wall or bench
3 × 10rest 60s4–7kg
Sit with your back fully supported against a wall or bench — removes the lower-back arch entirely. Ribs down, press straight up.
Sit on a chair or the floor with your back flat against a wall. Hold DBs at shoulder height, palms forward. Brace your core and press straight overhead. Lower back to shoulders with control — the wall support means no lumbar arch.
4
Bench Flylying · chest stretch
3 × 12rest 60s4–7kg
Soft elbows, open wide for a chest stretch, squeeze back together. Back stays flat on the bench.
Lie on the bench, DBs above your chest with a slight bend in the elbows. Open arms wide and lower until you feel a stretch across the chest, then squeeze the chest to bring them back together. Do not let the lower back arch off the bench.
5
Triceps Kickbackone arm, hand/knee on bench
3 × 15 /armrest 45s4kg light
Upper arm stays still and parallel to the floor — all the movement is at the elbow. Squeeze the triceps hard at full lockout.
Place one hand and the same-side knee on the bench for support, other foot planted on the floor. Hold the DB in your free hand with the upper arm pinned against your side and parallel to the floor, elbow bent 90°. Extend the arm straight back until fully locked out, keeping the upper arm still throughout, then return with control. Finish one arm before switching.
6
DB Lateral Raisesshoulder finisher · optional
2 × 15rest 45s4kg light
Lead with elbows, slow, no swinging. Trim this first if short on time.
Hold DBs at your sides with a slight bend in the elbows. Raise both arms out to the sides to shoulder height, leading with your elbows. Lower slowly — use a very light weight and avoid shrugging or swinging.
Core finisher · every session · Dead bug 2 × 8/side · Plank 2 × 30s. Press the lower back flat to the floor on the dead bug.
Back · rear delts · biceps · all dumbbells · ~25–30 min
Warm-up · 15 band pull-aparts · arm swings · shoulder rolls
1
Fast Dumbbell Rowschest on sofa back or braced hinge
4 × 5rest 60–90s7–10kg
Back-safe default: rest your chest on the back of a sofa to support the spine, or hinge with a hard-braced flat back. Start light. Pull fast to the ribs, control the lower.
Option A (safest): drape your chest over the back of a sofa, arms hanging down, and row both DBs up to your ribs fast. Option B: hinge forward with knees soft and back locked flat, brace hard, then pull. Never round the lower back.
2
DB Pulloverone DB, on bench · lats
3 × 12rest 60s4–7kg
The best dumbbell move for the lats (replaces the band pulldown). Ribs down, back flat, feel the stretch.
Lie on your back on the bench, holding one dumbbell in both hands above your chest with arms only slightly bent. Keeping that slight bend, lower the weight back over your head until you feel a stretch through the lats and chest, then pull it back over your chest. Keep the ribs down and the lower back flat — don't let it arch.
3
One-Arm DB Rowhand on bench · heaviest
3 × 10 /armrest 60s7–10kg · heaviest
Main horizontal pull — your strongest. Pull to the hip, flat back.
Place one hand and the same-side knee on the bench for support. Hold DB in the opposite hand, arm hanging down. Pull the DB up to your hip, keeping your back flat and elbow close to your body. Lower slowly.
4
Dumbbell Curlsbiceps
2 × 12rest 45s4–7kg
Slow, no swinging — keep the upper arms pinned to your sides.
Hold DBs at your sides, palms facing forward. Curl both up by bending at the elbows, keeping your upper arms pinned to your sides. Lower slowly back to the start.
5
DB Reverse Flyface-down · rear delts & posture
2 × 15rest 45s4kg light
The posture move (replaces face pulls). Lead with the elbows, flare them high toward your ears, squeeze the shoulder blades.
Lie face-down on a bench or the floor (or bend over with a hard-braced flat back), dumbbells hanging below the shoulders with a soft bend in the elbows. Raise both arms out and up, leading with the elbows and flaring them high toward your ears, squeezing the shoulder blades together at the top. Lower slowly. Keep it light.
6
DB Hammer Curlsbiceps & forearms · optional finisher
2 × 12rest 45s4–7kg
Palms facing each other throughout, no swinging — hits the forearms and the outer biceps.
Hold the dumbbells at your sides with palms facing each other (thumbs up). Curl both up keeping that neutral grip and your upper arms pinned to your sides, then lower slowly. Don't swing.
Core finisher · every session · Bird-dog 2 × 8/side · Side plank 2 × 20s/side. Slow and controlled — this one's all posture.

Kettlebell · power/cardio · carry · balance · core · ~25–30 min
Warm-up · 10 bodyweight hip hinges (no weight) · arm circles · 5 slow cat-cows
1
Kettlebell Swingspower + cardio · hip hinge
8 × 20s hardrest 40s12kg KB
Hinge, don't squat — flat back always, power comes from snapping the hips forward, not the arms lifting. You're new to this pattern: start light and slow, technique before intensity.
Feet shoulder-width, KB on the floor slightly in front of you. Hinge at the hips to grip it (flat back, like the start of an RDL), then hike it back between your legs. Snap your hips forward explosively to swing the KB up to chest height — the arms just guide it, they don't lift. Let it float weightless for a moment, then let it fall back into the next hinge. Keep the back flat throughout; if it rounds, stop and reset. This is your 4th power move alongside the squat jumps, dynamic-effort bench, and fast rows — and doubles as your cardio interval work.
2
KB Suitcase Carryone KB, one hand · grip & anti-lean
2 × 30m /siderest 45s12kg KB
One kettlebell, one hand, walk tall — don't lean away from it.
Hold the kettlebell at your side by the handle, standing tall with shoulders level and ribs down. Walk 30m, then swap hands. The work is resisting the sideways lean, not the walking — if your torso is tipping, the weight is too heavy for now (go back to a dumbbell). Grip failing before the distance is done is normal and is the point — this is the direct forearm and grip work the rest of the routine doesn't cover. No space to walk? March on the spot for 40s per side instead.
3
Single-Leg Stancebalance · progress to reach
3 × 30s /side
Stand near a wall or counter for safety at first. Quiet, still balance — minimal wobbling, hips level.
Stand on one leg, other foot lifted a few inches off the floor, hips level and core gently braced. Hold, staying as still as possible. Once 30s feels easy without touching down, progress by closing your eyes, or by adding a slow reach with the free foot — forward, out to the side, then back — before returning to centre each time. This is one of the best predictors of staying steady on your feet as you age; the goal is quality of balance, not effort.
4
Tandem Walkheel-to-toe · dynamic balance
2 × 10 steps
Walk a straight line, heel touching toe each step. Slow and controlled, arms out for balance if needed.
Walk forward in a straight line, placing the heel of each foot directly in front of the toes of the other, as if on a tightrope. Go slowly and use your arms for balance. Do 10 steps forward, then 10 steps backward the same way. Progress by turning your head side to side as you walk, once the basic version feels easy.
5
Pallof Haloanti-rotation · band or light DB
6 /side
Circle the weight around your head without letting your torso move.
Stand side-on to a door-anchored band (or hold a light dumbbell at your chest). Press it straight out in front of your sternum, arms locked. Now trace a slow circle around your head and back to the start. Your ribs, hips and spine should not move at all — only the arms. This is anti-rotation work: the whole point is resisting the pull, not producing movement. Go slow. If your torso twists or your hips shift, reduce the tension. Then repeat facing the other way.
6
Half-kneeling Woodchoprotation · one light DB
8 /side
Drive with the torso, not the arms. Hips stay square and still.
Kneel on one knee, other foot planted in front (half-kneeling). Hold one dumbbell in both hands beside the hip of the down leg. Draw it diagonally up and across your body to above the opposite shoulder, rotating through the ribcage — not the lower back. Control it back down on the same path. Keep the hips square and facing forward throughout; the rotation comes from the upper back. This is the rotation pattern the rest of the routine doesn't train. Light weight only — this is a control exercise, not a strength one. Swap the kneeling leg for the second side.
Back-safe guardrails · Flat back is non-negotiable on the swing and the carry — if you feel it in the lower back, lighten the load or shorten the range. Start the swing slow and low, and only add height and pace once the hinge feels automatic.
Important · Because back niggles recur, and the swing loads the hinge under speed, a physio check-in before ramping up the kettlebell is worth it. Stop any exercise immediately if it pinches, causes sharp pain, or sends pain down a leg — and see a professional before continuing.
Weekly shape: Legs · Push · Pull · KB & Core, repeating — with one rest day worked in wherever the week needs it. All four days now get roughly equal airtime, rather than this one trailing behind.
Core patterns: this tab covers rotation (woodchop), anti-rotation (Pallof halo), and anti-lateral-flexion (KB carry). Anti-extension comes from the dead bug + plank finisher in every main session, plus the hard bracing the swing itself demands to control the hip snap. Cardio/VO2max comes from the swing intervals.
